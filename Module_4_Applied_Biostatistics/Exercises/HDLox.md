# Background

HDLox refers to oxidized high-density lipoprotein (HDL). HDL is often
called "good" cholesterol because it typically helps remove excess
cholesterol from the bloodstream, reducing the risk of heart disease.
HDLox indicates that the HDL particles themselves have become oxidized.
This oxidation process can impair their ability to function properly,
potentially leading to:

- Reduced cholesterol efflux: HDL's primary role is to transport
  cholesterol from tissues back to the liver for removal. Oxidized HDL
  may be less effective at this process.

- Increased inflammation: Oxidized HDL can trigger inflammatory
  responses, which can contribute to the development of atherosclerosis
  (plaque buildup in the arteries).

- Impaired antioxidant activity: HDL normally has antioxidant
  properties, helping to protect cells from damage. Oxidation can
  diminish these antioxidant capabilities.

## Connection to CVD:

Research suggests a link between higher levels of HDLox and an increased
risk of cardiovascular disease (CVD), including:

- Heart attack

- Stroke

- Atherosclerosis

## Important Note:

HDL levels alone are not always a reliable predictor of heart disease
risk. HDL function is crucial. Even if HDL cholesterol levels are high,
if the HDL is significantly oxidized, it may not provide the same level
of protection against heart disease.

## Further Research:

Ongoing research is exploring the role of HDLox in CVD development and
the potential for therapeutic interventions that target HDL oxidation.
